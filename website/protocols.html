Run this pool
under your own name.

SAFU gives protocols, wallet providers, and platforms holding user funds a way to protect their users, individuals or autonomous agents alike, from phishing, approval exploits, and key compromise. An ecosystem can deploy the same pool one level up, protecting the protocols built on it instead: when one is hacked, the payout goes to the protocol itself, fast enough to stop a bank run before it starts. Neither flow has a vote or a claim form, because the contract enforces the payout rule itself.

// 01 — for protocols, wallet providers & platforms

Protect your own users' wallets.

You hold your users' funds, or your users hold their own wallet through your product. A verified drain triggers an automatic payout, up to a fixed multiple of the stake, sized to the wallet's risk tier and never more than the loss itself.

[ 01 ] Your pool, your brand.

Your users stake through your product and the pool carries your name. The audited engine underneath is ours, so you ship coverage without building a claims system to run it.

[ 02 ] Wallet-level loss covered.

Phishing, approval exploits, and key compromise — the ways a wallet actually gets drained, covering wallets run by people and by autonomous agents alike.

[ 03 ] No vote, no claim form.

The contract enforces the payout rule itself, and the same loss event always produces the same calculation. Nobody sits in a room deciding who deserves it.

[ 04 ] Nothing collected upfront.

Your users pay no premium. A staker who claims forfeits their principal, and that forfeiture is what funds the payout.

// 02 — for ecosystems & chains

Protect the protocols built on you.

A chain or a foundation deploys one level up — a pool that covers the protocols built on that ecosystem, not their end-users directly. It works like a reserve ratio: when a protocol in the pool is hacked, the payout goes to the protocol itself, deterministically and fast, so it can absorb the loss and keep honoring withdrawals. End-users are protected as a consequence of the protocol staying solvent, not as a separate direct claim.

[ 01 ] Stops a bank run before it starts.

The payout lands fast enough that the protocol keeps honoring withdrawals, so its users never see a locked balance.

[ 02 ] Protocol-level exploits covered.

Oracle and governance manipulation — the vectors an audit checks for but cannot insure against once one slips through.

[ 03 ] Member protocols stake in, not the ecosystem alone.

Protocols in the pool stake into a shared reserve, with optional seed capital from the launching ecosystem — the same forfeiture-funded mechanism as the wallet-level pools, one level up.

[ 04 ] Isolated per deployment.

Each pool is its own contract instance, capitalized independently. A new partner's pool never exposes another partner's stakers.

// 03 — deploy on your chain

Built once, ported per chain.

The engine already runs across two very different execution models — Ethereum's EVM and Stellar's Soroban — with the same deterministic mechanism underneath both. That is the proof it ports, not the limit of where it can go.

[ 01 ] One engine, not a rebuild.

The same deterministic scoring and payout mechanism runs on Ethereum's EVM and Stellar's Soroban today. A new chain is a deployment target, not a from-scratch build.

[ 02 ] Backed by a chain-specific audit.

Every new deployment goes through its own security review before it takes real stakes, matching the process both live chains already passed.

[ 03 ] Bring us your ecosystem.

Tell us which chain, and we scope what it takes to run a pool there, with no fixed roadmap promising a chain we haven't committed to yet.

// 04 — looking further out

A future model: audit pools.

Longer term, SAFU intends to let a protocol fund an audit and a coverage pool at once: instead of paying a one-time audit fee, the protocol deposits into a pool SAFU reviews and sizes, and that same pool pays users back if a bug slips through. This is not built or scoped today; it is the direction after the two models above prove out.
