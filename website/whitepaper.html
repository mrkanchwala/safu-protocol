Abstract

Every year, billions in crypto are drained from wallets and protocols through phishing attacks, stolen keys, and other exploits. The protocols designed to cover these losses require a committee to vote on your claim, or don't cover wallet-level drains at all.

SAFU gives protocols, wallets, and platforms the technology to socialize these losses deterministically, at the wallet level or the protocol level, with no premium and no vote. The same event always produces the same payout calculation. When a drain is verified, a 7-day cooldown starts, then a 45-day payout stream begins.

SAFUPoolV8 is live on Ethereum mainnet, with a Stellar testnet deployment carrying 184 passing tests. A protocol can deploy this same engine under its own brand, giving its own users deterministic coverage without building a claims system from scratch.

This document describes how the protocol works, how the economics hold, and where it is heading.

// 02

The Problem

Wallet-level drain is the most consistent attack surface in crypto. Trust Wallet's Chrome extension breach on December 24, 2025 drained thousands of wallets for millions of dollars within hours of a compromised update reaching users. Security losses held at $972 million across 207 incidents in the first half of 2026 alone, even as DeFi's total value locked fell 38 percent, so the underlying risk did not shrink with the market around it.

[ phishing ]
User signs a transaction that sweeps their wallet to a drain address. Often triggered by a fake website or malicious link.
[ approval exploit ]
A previously granted token approval is used to drain the wallet. No new signature required from the victim.
[ key compromise ]
The wallet's private key is exposed and everything in it gets moved out automatically. Nothing on-chain can stop it once the key is out.

Existing protocols don't cover this

Nexus Mutual settles claims by member vote, and its tracked value has fallen from about $800 million in 2022 to roughly $100 to 200 million today. InsurAce's value locked has dropped to $133,106 from a roughly $55 to 58 million peak, and Unslashed Finance's has fallen to $2.79 million from a roughly $170 to 180 million peak. None of these operate at the wallet level, and none let a business run the mechanism itself.

For the protocols that do attempt wallet-level coverage, every claim goes to a vote. Your payout depends on DAO participation rates you didn't choose. The result is slow, unpredictable, and discretionary.

The agent gap

AI agents managing capital on hot wallets cannot exercise human judgment. Coverage for these wallets is a runtime dependency, the same way gas is. Four major wallet providers, MetaMask, Coinbase, OKX, and BNB Chain, shipped agent-specific wallets within a single month in 2026. No existing coverage product serves this segment.

// 03

What SAFU Is

SAFU gives protocols, wallets, and platforms the technology to socialize losses deterministically, whether the loss hits an individual wallet or the protocol itself. The payout is calculated by code, and the same drain event always produces the same result, so there is nothing to argue about and no one to petition.

The protocol runs a fraud scanner against every submitted transaction hash. The scanner scores the transaction for drain patterns: sweep completeness, receiver classification, automation signals. That score, combined with the staker's tier and policy terms, produces one output: an entitlement amount, or a rejection reason.

A protocol can deploy this same engine under its own brand, giving its own users deterministic coverage without building a claims system from scratch.

The same drain event always produces the same payout calculation, every time. The calculation is the decision, and neither a vote nor a governance proposal can change it.

What this means for AI agents

An agent submits a transaction hash to the SAFU Verify API and receives a signed payout decision programmatically. No human in the loop on either side. Coverage becomes a runtime call, not a claims process.

How It Works

A wallet stakes any amount between 0.01 and 0.75 ETH to enroll. Enrollment is permissionless — no oracle approval required. The wallet's tier is assessed at claim time, not at staking. Tier determines the coverage multiplier applied to the staked amount.

TierCoverage multiplierMax payout (0.75 ETH stake)Wallet profile
A15x11.25 ETHClean history, low risk signals
B10x7.5 ETHMixed history, moderate signals
C5x3.75 ETHHigher risk signals detected

The staker sets a beneficiary address at enrollment. All payouts go to the beneficiary, never to the staked wallet. This protects the payout address from the same attack that triggered the claim.

Claim flow

01
Submit drain transaction
The staker submits the transaction hash of the drain event to the SAFU Verify API.
02
Fraud scanner scores it
The off-chain scanner checks sweep completeness, receiver classification, and automation signals. Score determines the entitlement amount.
03
Verification pipeline runs
Sequential checks: wallet enrolled and active, drain within coverage window, loss above minimum threshold, entitlement within tier cap. Pipeline short-circuits on first failure.
04
7-day cooldown
Approved claims wait 7 days before payout begins. Stake is permanently forfeited at submission.
05
45-day payout stream
Linear payout to beneficiary over 45 days. A dynamic outflow cap (5%/3%/1% per day by tier) prevents any single event from draining the pool.

Oracle and wallet safety

The payout oracle signing key is held in AWS KMS (secp256k1, eu-north-1), isolated from the server runtime. All signed messages include the contract address and chain ID, which prevents replay attacks. A 2-of-2 override mechanism (owner + co-signer, separate keys) handles disputes without bypassing rate limits.

Staking sends ETH to the pool and grants no token approvals and no spending permissions. The contract cannot access any other asset in your wallet, before, during, or after staking.

The fraud scanner is being expanded into a chain-parity module covering Ethereum, Solana, and Stellar detection patterns under one scoring backend, so the same engine that scores Ethereum drains today becomes the mechanism for every future chain SAFU deploys to, rather than a separate scanner per chain.

Economic Model

The Ethereum staking pool is ETH-only; each chain's pool is denominated in that chain's own native asset. Claims are paid from this pool. A dynamic outflow cap (5%/3%/1% per day by tier) and 45-day vesting period ensure no single event can drain it.

The initial deployment is a test pool designed to stress-test the system under real conditions. Coverage caps are small by design. Production economics require a larger pool and higher stake amounts, scoped for Q3 2026.

Why stake?

Stakers provide the pool capital that makes coverage possible. In return: wallet-drain protection while staked, with the ability to withdraw anytime if no claim is filed. Principal is returned in full when you withdraw without claiming.

Roadmap

01
Ecosystem integration
Grant-funded deployment and audit on each chain builds the credibility SAFU needs before any protocol on it will trust it. Stellar's build is already in motion under SCF #44, with the funded audit and mainnet deploy following as that grant's remaining tranches close.
02
Whitelabel pools
The whitelabel deployment package gets productized, reusing the same contracts and backend already live on Ethereum. A first pilot partner signs, and two to three additional deployments follow, targeting DAO treasuries specifically.
03
Case study and referral motion
First case study published using a live partner's real usage data. Formal ecosystem and partner referral motion begins.

Model 2 — B2C pools (deferred)

Longer term, once ecosystem integration and whitelabel pools produce real traction, SAFU intends a dedicated push into direct consumer reach for the same pool individuals and agents can already stake into today.

Model 3 — SAFU Audit protocol pools (deferred)

Longer term, SAFU intends to let a protocol deposit capital into a coverage pool instead of paying a traditional audit fee. SAFU's own team reviews the protocol's code, and the protocol's coverage level is set by that review and its size. No token exists today, and one would only be introduced if Model 2 or Model 3 materialize.

Legal

SAFU is not a financial product, insurance product, or investment contract. The protocol provides a deterministic verification service. Coverage depends on fraud scanner output and verification pipeline evaluation. No payout is guaranteed.

No SAFU token exists today, and none is being offered. If one is introduced alongside the deferred models described in the Roadmap, it would be a utility token conferring governance rights and access to protocol functions, not a security under any jurisdiction.
