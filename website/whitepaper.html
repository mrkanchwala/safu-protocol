Abstract

Every year, billions in crypto are drained from wallets and protocols through phishing attacks, stolen keys, and other exploits. The protocols designed to cover these losses require a committee to vote on your claim, or don't cover wallet-level drains at all.

SAFU gives protocols, wallets, and platforms the technology to socialize these losses deterministically, at the wallet level or the protocol level, with no premium and no vote. The same event always produces the same payout calculation. When a drain is verified, a 7-day cooldown starts, then a 45-day payout stream begins.

The protocol is live on multiple chains, each running the same audited engine against that chain's own native asset. A protocol can deploy this engine under its own brand, giving its own users deterministic coverage without building a claims system from scratch.

This document describes how the protocol works, how the economics hold, and where it is heading.

// 02

The Problem

Wallet-level drain is the most consistent attack surface in crypto. Trust Wallet's Chrome extension breach on December 24, 2025 drained thousands of wallets for millions of dollars within hours of a compromised update reaching users. Security losses held at $972 million across 207 incidents in the first half of 2026 alone, even as DeFi's total value locked fell 38 percent, so the underlying risk did not shrink with the market around it.

[ phishing ]
User signs a transaction that sweeps their wallet to a drain address. Often triggered by a fake website or malicious link.
[ approval exploit ]
A previously granted token approval is used to drain the wallet. No new signature required from the victim.
[ key compromise ]
The wallet's private key is exposed and everything in it gets moved out automatically. Nothing on-chain can stop it once the key is out.

Existing protocols don't cover this

Nexus Mutual settles claims by member vote, and its tracked value has fallen from about $780 million at its 2021 peak to roughly $86 million. InsurAce's value locked has dropped to $133,106 from a roughly $55 to 58 million peak, and Unslashed Finance's has fallen to $2.79 million from a roughly $170 to 180 million peak. None of these operate at the wallet level, and none let a business run the mechanism itself.

For the protocols that do attempt wallet-level coverage, every claim goes to a vote. Your payout depends on DAO participation rates you didn't choose. The result is slow, unpredictable, and discretionary.

The agent gap

AI agents managing capital on hot wallets cannot exercise human judgment. Coverage for these wallets is a runtime dependency, the same way gas is. Four major wallet providers, MetaMask, Coinbase, OKX, and BNB Chain, shipped agent-specific wallets within a single month in 2026. No existing coverage product serves this segment.

// 03

What SAFU Is

SAFU gives protocols, wallets, and platforms the technology to socialize losses deterministically, whether the loss hits an individual wallet or the protocol itself. The payout is calculated by code, and the same drain event always produces the same result, so there is nothing to argue about and no one to petition.

The protocol runs a fraud scanner against every submitted transaction hash. The scanner scores the transaction for drain patterns: sweep completeness, receiver classification, automation signals. That score, combined with the staker's tier and policy terms, produces one output: an entitlement amount, or a rejection reason.

A protocol can deploy this same engine under its own brand, giving its own users deterministic coverage without building a claims system from scratch.

The same drain event always produces the same payout calculation, every time. The calculation is the decision, and neither a vote nor a governance proposal can change it.

What this means for AI agents

An agent submits a transaction hash to the SAFU Verify API and receives a signed payout decision programmatically. No human in the loop on either side. Coverage becomes a runtime call, not a claims process.

How It Works

A wallet stakes into the pool to enroll, within the range that pool sets. Enrollment is permissionless — no oracle approval required. The wallet's tier is assessed at claim time, not at staking. Tier sets the maximum coverage a claim can pay against the staked amount. It is a ceiling, not a fixed amount: a verified claim pays up to that multiple, and never more. What a specific claim pays within that ceiling depends on the verified loss.

TierMaximum coverageWallet profile
Aup to 15xClean history, low risk signals
Bup to 10xMixed history, moderate signals
Cup to 5xHigher risk signals detected

The staker sets a beneficiary address at enrollment. All payouts go to the beneficiary, never to the staked wallet. This protects the payout address from the same attack that triggered the claim.

Security

Every deployment ships with a full test suite and an independent security review before it goes live.

Verified by symbolic execution

On the EVM implementation specifically, the following core invariants are additionally verified by symbolic execution (Halmos) across every possible contract state — a formal-methods check beyond the test suite and review above. Soroban has no equivalent symbolic verifier available today; per-chain verification status is shown on the homepage.

  • Claiming permanently forfeits your stake, enforced on-chain by the contract itself
  • Pool balance stays accurate when a claim is cancelled
  • Once a claim is filed, the original stake cannot be withdrawn
  • The two approval keys are always held by different addresses
  • Contract ownership cannot be transferred to the co-approval address
  • The contract never owes more than it holds in ETH
  • Payouts above coverage limits are always blocked
  • A cancelled claim can never release further payments
✓ on EVM: all listed properties verified across every possible contract state

Soroban has no tool equivalent to Halmos's symbolic execution yet, so its compensating control is different, not absent: 238 automated tests cover the same invariant surface (forfeiture on claim, balance accuracy, withdrawal locking, two-key separation, payout caps), and property-based fuzzing is designed as the project's second layer for what symbolic execution would otherwise catch.

Oracle and wallet safety

The payout oracle signing key is held in AWS KMS, isolated from the server runtime, using the signature scheme each chain requires. Every signed message is bound to a specific contract and network, which prevents replay. A 2-of-2 override mechanism (owner + co-signer, separate keys) handles disputes without bypassing rate limits.

Staking sends your asset to the pool and grants no token approvals and no spending permissions. The contract cannot access any other asset in your wallet, before, during, or after staking.

The fraud scanner runs on a chain-parity module, one scoring backend covering every chain SAFU deploys to, so the same engine that scores Ethereum drains today becomes the mechanism for the next chain rather than a separate scanner built from scratch.

Economic Model

Each chain's pool is denominated in that chain's own native asset. Claims are paid from this pool. A daily outflow cap, set per pool, and a 45-day vesting period ensure no single event can drain it.

The initial deployment is a test pool designed to stress-test the system under real conditions. Coverage caps are small by design. Production economics require a larger pool and higher stake amounts, scoped for Q3 2026.

Why stake?

Stakers provide the pool capital that makes coverage possible. In return: wallet-drain protection while staked, with the ability to withdraw anytime if no claim is filed. Principal is returned in full when you withdraw without claiming.

Before You Stake

Everything below is enforced by the contract. Read it before you stake.

Your beneficiary

  • You name a beneficiary when you stake, and it has to be a different address from the one you stake with. The contract rejects a stake that names itself.
  • The beneficiary is where your money goes. Both a claim payout and an ordinary withdrawal are sent there, so choose an address you control and can still reach later.
  • You sign every action from your staking wallet, and the funds arrive at the beneficiary. This is deliberate, not incidental: the contract never sends money to whichever key just signed a transaction. If your staking wallet is ever compromised, the attacker can trigger a withdrawal or a claim override, but the payout still lands only at your registered beneficiary — they gain nothing unless they also hold that separate key. It is the same protection SAFU exists to give you, applied to the contract's own custody of your funds.
  • You can change the beneficiary later from the same wallet you staked with.
  • Staking from a hot wallet while naming a cold wallet as beneficiary is supported, and it is the safer setup.

Staking and withdrawing

  • Connect a wallet the pool supports. The stake screen shows the minimum and maximum for the pool you are joining, and each pool is denominated in its own chain's native asset.
  • You can withdraw at any time while no claim of yours is open, and your full principal is sent to your beneficiary.
  • If the pool is ever paused, you can exit directly to your own staking wallet without waiting.

Filing a claim

  • A claim must be filed within 30 days of the incident.
  • If you have been staked for less than 90 days, your claim is still recorded. It is held until you reach 90 days, and nothing is forfeited while it waits.
  • Once you pass 90 days, you have 100 days to approve your own claim. If you do not, the claim expires, the reserved amount returns to the pool, and your stake stays yours.

Getting paid

  • After a claim activates there is a 7 day cooldown before anything can be paid.
  • Payment is then released steadily over 45 days. You collect it by signing from your staking wallet, and it arrives at your beneficiary.
  • The next collection always pays everything accumulated since the last one, so collecting weekly and collecting monthly reach the same total.
  • Collect at least once every 100 days. If a claim goes that long with no collection, anyone can close it and whatever you have not collected returns to the pool. Each collection resets the window, and since payment finishes releasing at day 45, one collection before the deadline gets you everything.
  • Each collection is its own transaction, so you can check every one on a block explorer.
  • The pool has a daily limit on total payouts, which can spread a single day's collection. It does not reduce the total you are owed.

Points

  • Staking earns points over time.
  • When a claim activates, your entire points balance burns, not just a portion of it.

What a paid claim costs you

  • When a claim pays out, the principal you staked stays in the pool permanently. That forfeited principal is what funds payouts, and it is why there is no premium and no fee to stake.

If you think the result is wrong

  • Assessment is automated. No person decides whether your loss qualifies, and a valid claim never waits on someone's approval.
  • If a claim is rejected and you believe that is wrong, use the feedback form in the top navigation to ask for a human review.
  • Approving a claim that way takes two separate signers, so no single person can authorise a payout alone.
  • A human review is a way to correct a rejection in your favour.

If a claim is found fraudulent

  • A claim that has activated can be reversed if it turns out to be fraudulent or a false positive.
  • Your stake is restored, and it is then locked for 365 days before it can be withdrawn.
  • A claim still waiting on the 90 day mark carries no such lock, because nothing was forfeited yet.
